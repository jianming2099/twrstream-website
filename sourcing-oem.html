How We Work

Discover → Source → Develop → Prepare

We do not treat sourcing as a list of factory links. The objective is to turn a product request into a clearer, reviewable and commercially workable program.

01

Discover

Define the product, intended use, target market and priorities.

02

Source

Identify options and compare product and supplier fit.

03

Develop

Coordinate samples, specifications, branding and packaging direction.

04

Prepare

Confirm commercial information and organize the requirements for production and export.

What Gets Confirmed

Facts before assumptions.

MOQ, price, lead time, tooling and documentation can change with configuration and quantity. They are confirmed for the actual project rather than presented as universal promises.

Product Configuration

Model, material direction, size, color, functional requirements and intended use.

Commercial Terms

MOQ, quotation basis, sample cost, payment requirements and validity.

Timeline

Sample preparation, revisions, production schedule and shipping-readiness milestones.

Branding & Packaging

Logo method, artwork, labels, inserts, retail packaging and carton requirements.

Quality Review

Inspection points, approved sample reference and issue-handling expectations.

Market Requirements

Destination market, channel requirements and product-specific documentation needs.

Practical Questions

Before a project begins.

What information is enough to begin?

A product image, link, category description or intended use can be enough for an initial review. Target market and quantity direction are helpful when available.

Can you confirm MOQ before reviewing the product?

MOQ depends on the exact configuration, supplier, branding and packaging requirements. It is confirmed after the request is reviewed.

Do you support private label projects?

Private label and OEM direction can be reviewed, but available options depend on the selected product and manufacturing setup.

Can you help with samples?

Sample availability, timing and cost are confirmed for the relevant product. Feedback and revision points can then be coordinated.
