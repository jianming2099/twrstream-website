How to Source Products Directly from China Without a Middleman

By TowerStream Interactive  ·  September 2026  ·  8 min read

Every year, thousands of retailers, content creators, and wholesale buyers pay more than they have to. Not because they lack options, but because they haven't found a direct path to the factory floor. A middleman adds a markup. Sometimes two middlemen add two markups. By the time a product reaches a buyer in the US or Europe, the factory price may be a fraction of what you paid.

This guide is for buyers who want to change that. Whether you run an e-commerce store, manage retail purchasing, or are building a private-label brand, factory-direct sourcing from China is one of the most powerful cost and quality levers available to you.

Why Buyers Still Use Middlemen

The honest answer: middlemen solve real problems. They handle language barriers, coordinate shipments, manage quality checks, and absorb the risk of dealing with an unfamiliar factory halfway around the world. That's worth something. But it costs something too, typically 10% to 30% added to the unit price, sometimes more.

The good news is that many of the problems middlemen solve are solvable directly, especially when you work with a US-based trade company that has built those factory relationships over decades and operates with Western business standards. That's a different arrangement from going it alone on Alibaba.

The Three Paths to Factory-Direct Sourcing

Path 1: Trade Shows and In-Person Visits

Canton Fair (held twice yearly in Guangzhou) remains the largest sourcing event in the world. If you can attend, it gives you direct access to thousands of manufacturers across every category. The limitation is cost and logistics — flights, hotels, translator fees, and the time to vet vendors in person add up fast.

Path 2: B2B Platforms

Alibaba, 1688 (China's domestic wholesale platform), and Global Sources are the most common starting points for buyers who can't travel. These platforms give you access to factory listings, but they require diligence: not every "factory" is a factory, MOQs (minimum order quantities) vary enormously, and communication can be inconsistent without a reliable contact on the ground.

Path 3: Working with a US-Based Trade Partner

This is the path that combines the cost advantage of factory-direct with the accountability of a US business relationship. A trade company like TowerStream Interactive has spent over 20 years building factory relationships in Zhejiang Province, one of China's most productive manufacturing hubs. When you work through a partner like this, you're not paying a traditional middleman markup. You're paying for verified access, quality control, and a company that answers to US business standards.

"The goal isn't to cut everyone out. It's to cut out the layers that add cost without adding value. A good trade partner earns their place in the chain."

What to Know Before You Start Sourcing

Minimum Order Quantities

Most Chinese factories operate on MOQs, the minimum number of units they'll produce per order. For established product categories, MOQs can range from 100 units on the low end to 5,000 or more for complex manufacturing. Understanding your volume before approaching a factory sets realistic expectations for both sides.

Product Categories and Specialization

China's manufacturing landscape is deeply specialized by region. Yiwu is famous for small commodities and accessories. Shenzhen dominates electronics. Zhejiang Province, where TowerStream's supply chain is rooted, is particularly strong in textiles, bags, apparel, jewelry findings, and home goods. Matching your product category to the right manufacturing region matters more than most buyers realize.

Quality Control

Factory-direct sourcing without quality oversight is a gamble. Before placing a large order, request samples. If possible, arrange a pre-shipment inspection through a third-party quality control firm operating in China (Asia Inspection and QIMA are well-known options). A small investment in quality control can prevent an expensive mistake at scale.

Shipping and Customs

Air freight is fast and expensive. Sea freight is slow and affordable. For most wholesale buyers, sea freight via a freight forwarder is the standard route. Factor in customs duties, which vary by product category and country of import. For US buyers, the Harmonized Tariff Schedule (HTS) codes determine your duty rate. Work with a licensed customs broker the first time you import, not guessing through this step.

Red Flags When Vetting Suppliers

  • They can't provide a business license or factory certification.
  • Sample quality differs significantly from production samples (a classic bait-and-switch).
  • They pressure you to wire a large deposit before any paperwork is in order.
  • They can't provide references from other Western buyers.
  • Communication response times are unpredictable or evasive on specifics.

A factory relationship that's been in place for years carries a level of accountability that a cold Alibaba contact simply doesn't. This is one of the core advantages of working through a trade partner with an established presence on the ground.
