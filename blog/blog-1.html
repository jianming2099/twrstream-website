Insights · Sourcing

How to source products from China without relying on assumptions.

Good sourcing is less about finding the lowest visible price and more about building a verifiable path from product brief to supplier, sample, commercial terms, production and export preparation.

A buyer can discover suppliers through trade shows, B2B platforms, industry networks or sourcing partners. Each route can work. The important distinction is between discovering a possible supplier and confirming that the supplier is suitable for a particular product, quantity, market and commercial requirement.

There is no universal factory-direct price, MOQ or lead time. These depend on product configuration, materials, customization, tooling, quantity, packaging, production schedule and destination requirements.

A practical sourcing sequence

01 · Define the brief

Clarify the product, target market, expected quantity direction, intended customer, reference images and customization requirements.

02 · Identify relevant suppliers

Shortlist manufacturers or supply partners whose product category and capabilities appear relevant to the brief.

03 · Verify the details

Confirm specifications, materials, MOQ, pricing basis, samples, lead time, packaging, payment terms and available documentation.

04 · Review before scaling

Use samples, specifications and agreed quality checkpoints to reduce ambiguity before production decisions are made.

Supplier discovery is not supplier verification

A marketplace listing, catalogue or factory introduction can help identify candidates, but buyers should still establish who they are dealing with and what is actually being offered. Business information, product capability, quotation scope and documentation should be checked in the context of the project.

Questions worth confirming

  • What exact product configuration does the quotation cover?
  • Which materials, dimensions, finishes and packaging are included?
  • What is the MOQ for the selected configuration and customization?
  • What sample options and costs apply?
  • When is lead time counted from, and what must be approved first?
  • Which test reports, certificates or other documents are available for this specific product?
  • What shipping terms and export preparation are included or excluded?

Use samples to close information gaps

A sample can help evaluate appearance, construction, usability, packaging and whether the supplier interpreted the brief correctly. For customized products, the sample or pre-production approval process is also where artwork, colors, logo placement and packaging details can be aligned.

Sampling does not automatically prove that every production unit will be identical, so larger or higher-risk projects may also require agreed inspection criteria or independent quality-control support.

Plan compliance around the destination market

Documentation requirements differ by product and destination. Rather than assuming that a generic certificate is sufficient, identify the relevant market requirements and confirm what evidence is available for the exact product being considered. Specialized regulatory, customs or legal questions should be reviewed with qualified professionals where appropriate.
