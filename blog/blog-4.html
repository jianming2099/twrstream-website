How to Start a Wholesale Partnership with a US-Based Trade Company

By TowerStream Interactive  ·  September 2026  ·  8 min read

Wholesale partnerships should not be complicated. They fail when buyers and suppliers have mismatched expectations, unclear terms, or a communication gap between what was promised and what was delivered. They work when both sides know exactly what the relationship involves from the first conversation.

This guide walks through the practical process of starting a wholesale partnership with a US-registered trade company. Specifically, how TowerStream Interactive approaches these conversations, what a new wholesale partner can expect, and how to evaluate whether a trade partner is the right fit before you place your first order.

Why a US-Based Trade Company Is Different from a Chinese Supplier

When you work directly with a Chinese factory through a platform like Alibaba, you're entering a relationship governed by Chinese business norms, Chinese legal frameworks, and communication in a second language for at least one party. This works well for experienced buyers who know what questions to ask. For buyers who are newer to China sourcing, the gaps in accountability can be costly.

A US-registered trade company like TowerStream Interactive operates differently. We're accountable under US law. We communicate in English, using the business conventions North American and European buyers are accustomed to. When something goes wrong — and in manufacturing, things occasionally do — the resolution process is clear and follows standards you already understand.

The supply chain relationship with Chinese factories is ours to manage. That's what 20 years of factory relationships in Zhejiang Province gives us: direct access, without the middleman overhead, combined with the accountability structure of a US business.

The Process: From First Contact to First Order

  1. 1
    Initial Inquiry Send us a brief summary of what you're looking to source: product category, target quantity, market (retail, e-commerce, wholesale), and timeline. No detailed spec required at this stage. We're trying to understand whether what you need falls within what we can provide, which covers bags and accessories (TETCHY), home goods and appliances (LOOPBOSS), and jewelry and apparel (ShaiNewi).
  2. 2
    Discovery Call If there's a clear fit, we schedule a call to go deeper: your specific product requirements, quality expectations, pricing targets, and order cadence. This is where we determine whether we can meet your needs within a structure that works commercially for both parties. No commitment on either side at this point.
  3. 3
    Sampling For new wholesale relationships, we recommend starting with samples before any production order. Samples let you verify quality, dimensions, materials, and finish before committing to volume. Sample costs vary by product and are typically applied against your first production order. Turnaround time for samples ranges from 1 to 3 weeks depending on the product.
  4. 4
    Pricing and Terms Once samples are approved, we provide a formal pricing sheet with unit costs at your order volume, minimum order quantities, payment terms, and estimated lead times. Our standard payment structure is a deposit on order placement with the balance due before shipment — a normal arrangement for international wholesale that protects both parties.
  5. 5
    Production and Quality Control We manage production directly through our factory relationships. Before shipment, we conduct quality inspection at the factory level. For larger orders, we can coordinate third-party inspection if your company requires it. You'll receive production updates and confirmation before goods ship.
  6. 6
    Shipping and Delivery We work with established freight forwarders for sea and air shipments to North America and Europe. We provide all necessary export documentation. For buyers new to importing, we can recommend licensed customs brokers in your country who handle the import clearance process.

What We Look for in a Wholesale Partner

Not every inquiry becomes a long-term partnership, and that's fine. The relationships that work well over years share a few common characteristics.

Clear Product Requirements

The clearest wholesale relationships start with a buyer who knows what they want. This doesn't mean you need a technical specification on day one, but it does mean having a reasonable sense of the product, the quantity, and the end market. Buyers who are still figuring out their product strategy tend to have more friction in the sampling and approval process.

Realistic Volume Expectations

Factory-direct sourcing works best at volumes that justify production runs. For most of TowerStream's product categories, minimum order quantities start in the range of 100 to 500 units, depending on product complexity. If your initial order is significantly below that, we'll be upfront about whether it makes sense to proceed.

A Long-Term Perspective

The best wholesale partnerships are built on repeat orders, not one-off transactions. Factories allocate production capacity to customers who reorder consistently. Buyers who approach the relationship as a long-term supply arrangement, rather than a single transaction, get better pricing, faster turnaround, and priority production slots over time.

"The first order is where you learn whether the relationship works. The second order is where it starts to be worth something to both parties."

Common Questions from New Wholesale Buyers

Do I need an import license?

In the United States, most businesses do not need a specific import license to import goods commercially. You'll need a customs broker to file the entry and pay applicable duties, and you'll need an Employer Identification Number (EIN) for the customs declaration. For European buyers, the requirements vary by country, and a licensed customs broker in your country will guide you through the local requirements.

How do duties and tariffs affect my cost?

This is a real question that deserves a real answer. US tariffs on Chinese goods have fluctuated significantly in recent years, and the rate applicable to your product depends on its Harmonized Tariff Schedule (HTS) code. Before finalizing your landed cost calculation, look up the current tariff rate for your specific product category. Your customs broker can help with this. We can advise on which product categories have been affected and which have not, based on what we ship regularly.

What happens if there's a quality issue?

We stand behind what we ship. If a quality issue is identified before delivery, we don't ship it. If an issue is discovered after delivery that falls within the scope of what was agreed in our specification, we'll work with you on resolution — whether that's a credit toward the next order, replacement units, or a refund on the affected goods. The specifics depend on the nature of the issue and are addressed in our wholesale agreement terms.

Can I get exclusivity in my market?

Market or territory exclusivity is something we're open to discussing for established partners with sufficient order volume. For new relationships, we typically start without exclusivity and revisit the question once there's a track record of orders and a genuine commercial case for locking in a territory.
